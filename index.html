How it works

Three steps, and the mail never leaves your desk.

1

Connect your mailbox

Sign in with Google and grant read-only access. Reclaim cannot send, modify or delete anything — the permission it asks for makes that impossible, not merely forbidden.

2

Scan on your own machine

It reads message headers — who sent it and when — looking for the signatures of an account being created. The scan runs locally and writes its findings to a database file on your computer.

3

Exercise your rights

For each company, generate a request under Article 15 or Article 17. Reclaim tracks who has replied, who is late, and what you are still owed.

Privacy

A tool about your data shouldn't take any.

The point of Reclaim is finding out who holds your information. It would be absurd to become one of them, so the storage layer is built so it cannot.

Kept, on your device

  • The company's domain and display name
  • The first and last date it appeared
  • A confidence score for the match
  • Your sign-in token, in your operating system's keychain

Never stored, anywhere

  • Message bodies
  • Subject lines
  • Attachments
  • Message identifiers, contacts and recipients
Enforced in the code, not promised in a policy. There is no column anywhere in the database capable of holding a message body, and no server to receive one — the app talks to Google and to nothing else. The full detail is in the privacy policy.

Your rights

Two articles do most of the work.

The GDPR already gives you these rights over every company in that list. Reclaim's job is making them practical to use a hundred times instead of once.

Article 15

Right of access

Ask a company for everything it holds on you, where it got it, who it has shared it with, and how long it intends to keep it. Most people never find out; the right has existed the whole time.

Article 17

Right to erasure

Require a company to delete your personal data when it no longer has a reason to hold it — the account you opened once in 2017 to read one article being a fairly clear example.

One month to reply. Article 12(3) gives a company a calendar month to respond. Reclaim tracks that deadline for every request and tells you who has run out of time.
