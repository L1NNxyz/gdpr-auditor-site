What it does

Over years of using the web you sign up to hundreds of services and forget nearly all of them. Each one still holds your personal data. The evidence is sitting in your inbox: the "verify your email", the "welcome to", the password reset you never asked for.

The Account Auditor reads those signals from your Gmail account and produces a list of the companies that hold data about you, with the date each account first appeared. From there you can generate a request under Article 15 (access) or Article 17 (erasure) of the GDPR.

How your email is handled

Email bodies never leave your device, and are never written to disk. This is enforced in the code, not promised in a policy:

  • The app requests Gmail's read-only permission and nothing else. It cannot send, modify or delete mail.
  • It asks Google only for message headers — sender, subject, date — never message content.
  • The data structures that carry a message have no field capable of holding a body.
  • Everything is stored in a database file on your own computer. There is no server to receive it.

The full detail is in the privacy policy.
