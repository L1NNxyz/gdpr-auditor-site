Summary

The GDPR Account Auditor runs entirely on your own computer. It reads your Gmail with read-only permission, extracts the names of companies you hold accounts with, and stores that list in a file on your device.

No email content is transmitted to us or to anyone else. We operate no server that receives your data, and we have no access to it.

Who is responsible

The application is software you run yourself. Because your data stays on your device and is never sent to us, we do not act as a data controller or processor in respect of the email data you process with it. You remain in control of that data throughout.

Questions about this policy can be sent to the contact address shown on the consent screen when you sign in.

What the application accesses

Google account permission

The application requests a single Google permission:

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.readonly

This grants read-only access to Gmail messages and settings. It does not permit sending, modifying or deleting mail, and the application contains no code capable of doing so. No other Google permission is requested.

What it reads

The application asks Gmail to return only these message headers:

  • From — to identify the sending company
  • Subject — to recognise mail that indicates an account (see below)
  • Date — to record when an account first appeared
  • List-Unsubscribe — to distinguish automated senders
  • DKIM-Signature and Authentication-Results — to identify which company actually sent a message, so several sending addresses collapse into one company

Message bodies are never requested. Where a message is ambiguous, the application may read Gmail's short preview snippet to decide whether it indicates an account. That snippet is used to compute a score and is then discarded: it is not stored, not logged, and not transmitted.

Which mail it looks at

Before anything is read, Gmail is asked to return only messages whose sender or subject suggests an account. Everything else in your mailbox is never examined. The application looks for:

Kind of mailWhy it indicates an account
Signup and verification — verify your email, welcome to, activate your account, reset your password Sent when an account is created
Security — verification code, security code, sign-in attempt, new device, two-factor A code is issued to an account holder; there is no mailing-list version
Subscriptions and membership — your subscription, your plan, renewal, free trial, membership, loyalty, rewards A subscription or membership is an account
Account management — change your password, your profile, your settings Presupposes a profile already exists
Purchases — your order, order confirmation, receipt, invoice Suggests a customer relationship, though a guest checkout leaves no account
Delivery — has shipped, out for delivery, tracking Weakest signal, and treated as such
Policy updates — terms of service, privacy policy Sent to account holders, though also to subscribers
Sender address — no-reply@, accounts@, billing@, orders@, member@ Indicates automated mail from a company rather than a person

Each match adds to a score, and a company is only listed when the score passes a threshold. The score, and the reasons behind it, are shown in the application under How sure. This list is part of the application's source code and can be read there in full.

What is stored, and where

Everything is stored on your own device, in a database file inside your user account's application data directory.

The sign-in token is held in your operating system's keychain — Windows Credential Manager, macOS Keychain, or the Linux secret service — and is never written to a plain file.

What is never stored

  • Message bodies, in whole or in part
  • Subject lines
  • Attachments
  • Message identifiers
  • Contacts, recipients, or your address book
  • Your Google password, which the application never sees

The application writes no diagnostic log containing email addresses: where an address would appear in a log line, it is replaced by a one-way hash before that line is written.

Looking up a company's privacy contact

Companies must publish a contact for data requests, but there is no central list of them. If you press Find it for me on a company, the application opens that company's own privacy page and looks for the address.

This is the only time the application contacts anything other than Google, and it happens only when you press that button.

  • One company at a time, never in bulk and never during a scan.
  • Only pages the company publishes for exactly this purpose.
  • Nothing about you is sent: no identifier, no mailbox data, and not which other companies you hold accounts with. The request carries no cookies and no referrer.
  • Nothing is saved until you choose an address from what it found.

The alternative was asking you to open those same pages in your own browser. This makes the same request, carrying less.

You never have to use it. Links to open those pages yourself are shown alongside.

Sharing and disclosure

None. The application performs no analytics, sends no telemetry, and contains no advertising or tracking code. Apart from the contact lookup described above, which runs only on your instruction, it makes no network request other than to Google's Gmail API to read your mail and to Google's OAuth endpoints to sign you in.

We do not sell, rent or share your data, because we never receive it.

Retention and deletion

Data is kept until you delete it, and you control both stores:

  • The account list — delete the application's database file, or use the application's own delete function.
  • The sign-in token — sign out within the application, which removes the entry from your keychain.
  • Google's permission — revoke access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. This takes effect immediately and independently of the application.

Limited Use disclosure

The application's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Specifically, data obtained from Gmail is used solely to identify services you hold accounts with, for your own use within the application. It is not transferred to any third party, not used for advertising, not used to train any model, and not read by any human.

Children

The application is not directed at children under 16 and should not be used by them without the consent of a parent or guardian.
