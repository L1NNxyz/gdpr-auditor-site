2. The short version

The application processes your email entirely on your own device. There is no server that receives it. We do not receive your mail, nor the list of accounts derived from it, and we have no access to either at any point.

This policy therefore separates two things, which are legally quite different:

  • This website (sections 3 to 5) — where we process a small amount of personal data, essentially your IP address when you load a page.
  • The application (sections 6 to 9) — where you process your own data on your own device. We are not the controller for that, because we have no access to it and determine neither the purposes nor the means of that processing.

6. The application: processing on your device

The GDPR Account Auditor is software you run on your own computer. With your permission it reads headers from your Gmail messages, works out which companies you hold accounts with, and stores that list in a file on your device.

Our role: we provide the software but operate no service that receives your data. We determine neither the purposes nor the means of the processing of your email data, and we have no technical access to it. We are accordingly neither a controller nor a processor in respect of it. Where you use the application purely for personal purposes, your own processing also falls under the household exemption in Art. 2(2)(c) GDPR.

Google permission

The application requests a single Google permission:

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.readonly

This grants read-only access to messages and settings. It does not permit sending, modifying or deleting mail, and the application contains no code capable of doing so. No other permission is requested.

You sign in directly with Google. We never see your password. The access token is held in your operating system's credential store, not in a file.

You can revoke access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. That takes effect immediately and independently of this application.

What is read

The application asks Gmail to return only these headers:

HeaderPurpose
FromTo identify the sending company
SubjectTo recognise mail that indicates an account
DateTo record when an account first appeared
List-UnsubscribeTo distinguish automated senders
DKIM-SignatureTo collapse several sending addresses into one company
Authentication-ResultsThe same, where the signature is unavailable

Where the headers leave a message genuinely ambiguous, the application additionally reads the short preview line Gmail provides. That preview is scored and immediately discarded: it is not stored, not logged, and not transmitted.

What is stored on your device

  • Company domain (e.g. example.com)
  • Company display name
  • First and last date seen
  • A confidence score indicating how clear the match was
  • Which rules matched

What is never stored

  • Message bodies, in whole or in part
  • Subject lines
  • Attachments
  • Message identifiers
  • Contacts, recipients, or your address book
  • Your Google password, which the application never sees

The data structures that carry a message inside the application have no field capable of holding its content. This is not a rule someone has to remember; it is a property of the program.

The application writes no log containing email addresses: where an address would appear in a log line, it is replaced by a one-way hash before that line is written.

7. Looking up a company's privacy contact

Companies must publish a contact for data requests, but there is no central list of them. The application can read a company's own privacy page and look for the address — either for a single company, or for every company in your list at once.

This is the only time the application contacts anything other than Google, and it happens only when you ask it to.

  • Only when you press a button. Never automatically, never after a scan without being asked, and never in the background.
  • You can stop it at any point, and whatever it has found is kept.
  • One company at a time, in sequence, so no company receives a burst of requests.
  • Only pages the company publishes for exactly this purpose — their privacy policy, privacy notice or contact page.
  • Nothing about you is sent. No identifier, no mailbox data, and not which other companies you hold accounts with. The requests carry no cookies and no referrer.
  • A company is never contacted twice. Anything already having a contact or a self-service portal is skipped.

The alternative was asking you to open those same pages in your own browser. This makes the same requests, carrying less.

Addresses found this way are marked unchecked, because reading a page is not the same as confirming an address. You never have to use the feature at all: links to open those pages yourself are shown alongside.

8. Optional: a language model on your own computer

Deciding whether an email indicates an account, and finding a contact address written into a sentence rather than printed as a link, are reading tasks that pattern matching does badly. The application can use a small language model for those two jobs — but only one that is already installed on your own computer.

No model ships with the application, and nothing is downloaded or started on your behalf.

  • Detected, never provided. If suitable software with a model is running locally, the application uses it. If not — the ordinary case — it works entirely from its published scoring rules.
  • It runs on your machine. What is sent goes to a program listening on 127.0.0.1 on your own computer, and leaves the device at no point.
  • What it is shown: the subject line and sender of a message the scoring rules could not decide, or the visible text of a company privacy page already fetched.
  • Nothing is kept. The application stores the answer — a yes or no, or an address — and never the text it was shown.
  • No hosted service is used for this, and none can be configured. Subject lines are your mail; and the sequence of privacy pages the application reads would by itself tell an outside provider which companies you hold accounts with.

Nothing here is used to train anything. It is inference on your own hardware: no data is contributed to any model, by us or by anyone else.

Application data is kept until you delete it, and both stores are under your control:

Server logs for this website are deleted by the hosting provider according to its own schedule; we have neither influence over nor access to them.

10. Recipients and disclosure

None. The application performs no analytics, sends no telemetry, and contains no advertising or tracking code. Apart from the contact lookup described in section 7, which runs only on your instruction, it makes no network request other than to Google's Gmail API to read your mail and to Google's OAuth endpoints to sign you in. Where a local language model is in use, it also connects to 127.0.0.1 on your own computer; that traffic reaches no network and no other party.

We do not sell, rent or share your data, because we never receive it.
